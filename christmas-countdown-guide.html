Christmas Countdown Guide

Read the timer and turn festive milestones into a relaxed plan for December.

Updated 7 November 2026

Read all five boxes together

The countdown runs towards the start of 25 December in your device’s local time. Read the boxes as one duration: weeks, then remaining days, then hours, minutes and seconds. A display of three weeks and two days means 23 complete days, followed by whatever time appears in the smaller units.

The days box resets after six because seven complete days are represented as a week. Seconds roll into minutes, minutes into hours, and whole 24-hour periods into days. The separate sleeps message rounds the remaining duration up, so it can be larger than the complete days represented in the grid. See how sleeps are calculated for worked examples.

100 days: a gentle starting point

16 September is 100 calendar days before 25 December. Use this as an optional planning marker, rather than a signal to start buying everything. Make a short list of people you hope to see, work out which journeys require advance thought, and decide which parts of last year you would enjoy repeating.

A useful early decision is what to simplify. If an activity took more effort than enjoyment last year, change it or leave it out. A countdown should make the season easier to anticipate, rather than turn every remaining day into a deadline.

Two months and one month to go

25 October and 25 November are two calendar months and one calendar month before Christmas. Months have different lengths, so these milestones are not the same as fixed 60-day and 30-day durations. The timer itself measures elapsed time, not calendar months.

Around these points, confirm arrangements with the people involved. Keep a shared note of food contributions or travel plans if that helps. For parcels, bookings and local events, check the current details directly with the relevant provider; a general Christmas countdown cannot supply delivery cut-offs or opening times.

Advent and 1 December are different markers

Many shop-bought Advent calendars start on 1 December and continue through Christmas Eve. In many Western Christian traditions, Advent begins on the fourth Sunday before Christmas, so its starting date varies. A calendar with numbered doors and a church’s Advent season therefore do not necessarily begin together.

For a home countdown, choose a rhythm that suits you. Twenty-four paper slips can each contain a small activity, or four weekly notes can make a quieter alternative. An activity can be as modest as making a warm drink together or looking through old photographs.

One week: 18 December

18 December is seven calendar days before Christmas Day. This is a useful point to review practical essentials: who is coming, what needs preparing, and which plans can still be made simpler. Leave some space for rest and unexpected changes.

If you are cooking, write down the order of tasks instead of trying to remember everything. If you are visiting, confirm the time and what you have offered to bring. The goal is to reduce decisions on the day, not create a perfect timetable.

Christmas Eve, Christmas Day and beyond

24 December is Christmas Eve. The timer is still counting towards midnight, even for households whose main meal or gift exchange happens that evening. On 25 December it displays a Christmas greeting all day. On 26 December it begins counting towards next Christmas.

After the celebration, save a few notes for next year: a recipe that worked, a decoration that needs repair, or a tradition people particularly enjoyed. Then let the countdown run quietly until you want it again. Our world traditions guide shows why the festive season can have several meaningful dates.
